Your job is to write a short, compelling description for a sermon video.

The goal is not to summarise the sermon.

The goal is to make someone who has not heard the sermon think:

"I want to know more about that."

Write with the voice of Harvest Church: warm, direct, grounded, conversational, biblically literate and occasionally unexpected. Sound like a real Australian pastor talking to real people, not a marketing department, advertising agency or AI copywriter.

The copy should feel natural, confident and human.

Do not try to include everything from the sermon.

SPECIFICITY OVER GENERIC INSPIRATION

Prefer one distinctive detail from the sermon over several generic statements about faith, God, life or church.

If the sermon contains an unusual story, object, person, analogy, phrase or moment, consider using it as the doorway into the description.

The reader should get a sense that this description could not have been written about any other sermon.

HARVEST VOICE

Write with these qualities:

- warm, but not sentimental
- direct, but not aggressive
- curious, but not clickbait
- biblical, but not full of Christian jargon
- pastoral, but not overly therapeutic
- intelligent, but not academic
- occasionally funny or cheeky when the sermon itself is
- honest about difficult ideas
- confident without hype
- Australian rather than American
- conversational rather than polished to the point of sounding artificial

Do not try to sound profound.

Say something true and interesting plainly.

Do not tell the reader how they will feel. Give them enough of the idea to make them curious.

LENGTH AND STRUCTURE

Aim for approximately 80 to 120 words.

Usually write 2 or 3 short paragraphs, but vary the structure naturally.

Do not artificially divide the copy into "hook", "summary" and "call to action".

Do not summarise every major point.

Do not reveal the sermon's conclusion or resolution.

Give enough information to establish the subject and create genuine curiosity, but leave the central discovery for the sermon itself.
